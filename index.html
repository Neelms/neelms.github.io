Pterosaur-inspired robot

Robotics Intern · Jan 2026 – Present

Problem

A 100 lb robot modeled on Quetzalcoatlus lawsoni has to launch off all four limbs and flap its wings, which takes high torque in two degrees of freedom at the shoulder.

What I did

  • Estimated the torque and power needed for launch and flight, and assessed flapping mechanisms against them.
  • Designed a bevel-gear differential shoulder that lets two smaller motors combine their torque across two degrees of freedom, and specified its gears, motors, and bearings.
  • Designed a belt-and-pulley elbow drive and the full-scale wing prototype.
  • Wrote a Python tool for AGMA bending and contact stress checks that asks for each input once and returns factors of safety.
  • Modeled the robot in MuJoCo and use Model Predictive Control to drive a quadrupedal launch.

Semantic 3D mapper

Self-taught 3D computer vision · Oct 2026 – Present

Goal

Turn RGB-D camera video into a 3D map you can search with text: type "red chair" and the chair lights up in 3D.

What I did

  • Matched depth, color, and pose streams by timestamp, then back-projected each depth pixel with the pinhole camera model and transformed the points into the world frame.
  • Fused three full TUM RGB-D sequences into colored point clouds and downsampled them with 1.25 cm voxels.
  • Benchmarked two learned depth models, Depth Anything V2 and Apple Depth Pro, against sensor depth, and I'm calibrating their scale across three TUM sequences.

Next

Build the map from camera images alone with predicted depth, then add SAM and CLIP semantics and real SLAM poses.

Baja SAE steering rack and pinions

Design Engineer · 2024 – 2025 · First-place UTV

Initial design

  • Video showed drivers changing grip to reach full steering lock, so I increased the pinions' pitch diameter to reduce steering-wheel angle.
  • Estimated a 100 ft-lb maximum pinion torque from wheel-force transducer data.
  • Validated tooth geometry in KISSsoft against last year's gears, and the primary pinion's sleeve in ANSYS: factor of safety 1.7 at 100 ft-lb.

Mid-season redesign

  • A suspension geometry change altered the loads, so I redesigned the pinions in three days against a 4,000 lb tie-rod impact case.
  • Introduced a gear ratio between the primary and intermediate pinions, a first for the team.
  • Taught myself Siemens NX, ANSYS, and GD&T, and released 10+ GD&T drawings for steering, brake, and frame parts.

Mini Baja RC car

Built with three teammates in 10 days · First place

Problem

Fit a steering system into a small all-terrain RC car where the four-wheel drive, front differential, and frame all compete for the same space, and constrain it so it only moves the way it should.

What I did

  • Designed the steering: a servo arm and a mirrored second pivot, tied together by a center linkage so both arms pivot together and the tie rods push evenly.
  • Ran the tie rods on heim joints at both ends, with all three linkages joined by a single through-bolt.
  • Designed the front and rear uprights with a constant cross-section for strength and printability, housing two bearings behind a retaining lip.

Biomimetic worm robot

Robotics Hardware Engineering Intern · May – Aug 2025

Problem

The robot drooped heavily when crossing gaps longer than one segment, and it needed safe mounts for its camera, IMU, and servo driver.

What I did

  • Researched TPU and ran 200+ print-test iterations on the ligaments, finding that print speed, not infill or wall count, controlled their stiffness.
  • Measured droop with Tracker video analysis before and after the change.
  • Designed two-part camera and IMU holders that also carry the servo driver and ligament attachment points.
  • Built a portable working segment for a demo: PLA and TPU prints, an Arduino Nano, a PCA9685 servo driver, and a PowerBoost 1000C to run the servos at 5 V and make it rechargeable. I did all the soldering and wiring.
  • Ran head-leveling tests off a ledge, which showed the middle servos needed more torque.

Closed-loop motor controller

Controls Engineering Intern (co-op) · Aug – Dec 2025

Problem

A high-RPM cell filtration chamber needed precise speed profiles: duty cycling, sine waves, and gentle ramps to protect the cells and the magnetic torque coupling. As cells moved outward during spin-up, the chamber's inertia changed. Open-loop control worked on day one but couldn't hold those profiles.

What I did

  • Taught myself encoder decoding, filtering, and PID control, then built the loop from scratch on an Arduino, motor driver, and BLDC motor.
  • Decoded the Hall-effect quadrature encoder into live shaft position and speed.
  • Added a first-order Butterworth filter and wrote a custom PID loop.
  • Debugged the encoder signal and circuit on an oscilloscope, and tested against my own duty-cycle, sine, and ramp profiles until it tracked them smoothly without retuning.
